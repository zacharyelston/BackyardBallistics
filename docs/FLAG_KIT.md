# Flag kit

One flag per hole, about $6. The pole is the hole; a trash can lid at its base is optional and only adds a clang.

## Parts

| Part | Spec | Approx. cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pole | 1×2 furring strip, 8, 10 or 12 ft as the lumber yard sells them. The course designer chooses. | $3–$6 |
| Tip band | Bright orange tape, top 4 in of the pole | $1 |
| Guy lines | Three lengths of string, each about 1.25 × the pole height | $1 |
| Stakes | Three tent stakes | from a cheap pack |
| Telltales | Flagging tape, three short strips per line | from a roll |
| Windsock | Small sock on a swivel, mounted in the top quarter of the pole | $3 |
| Lid (optional) | Trash can lid, 22–24 in across, laid rim up; audible feedback only | free–$10 |

## Build

1. Wrap the top 4 in of the pole in orange tape. This is the tip the app sights against the sky.
2. Mount the windsock in the top quarter of the pole so it can swing freely without hiding the orange band.
3. If you use a lid, drill a hole in its centre just big enough for the pole (a 1×2 is about 0.75 × 1.5 in).
4. Tie the three guy lines at three-quarters of the pole height (6 ft on an 8 ft pole).
5. Tie flagging tape on each guy line at about 2, 4 and 6 ft above the ground.

## Set up a hole

1. Stand the pole where the hole goes (through the lid, if you use one), **resting on the ground**. Do not drive it in: the full height must stay visible.
2. Stake the three guy lines 120° apart, 1.5–2 m from the pole.
3. Open the app → **Lean check**. Hold the phone's back flat on the pole, top up, and record face 1. Move to the opposite face and record face 2. Adjust the lines until it reads **under 0.5°** ("Plumb").
4. Open **Measure pole height**. Stand 2–3 m away, tap the base, then the orange tip. Repeat from another side; the two should agree within 2 cm. Tap **Use as pole height**.
5. Record the pole base's GPS point for the course file (milestone 3 adds this to the app).

## Notes

- A 2° lean shifts the hole's ground point about 6 cm from the point under the tip. Under 0.5° keeps it near 2 cm.
- A round in the ring (within 1 m of the pole unless the course sets another) is a hit. Hitting the lid or the pole is just a clang. A potato that hits a guy line is played where it stops.
- Taller poles are easier to spot and range more precisely; 10 and 12 ft poles suit longer holes.
- The telltales also mark the lines so nobody trips on them.
